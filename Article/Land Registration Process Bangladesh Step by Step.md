# How to Register Land in Bangladesh as an NRB: A Step-by-Step Guide

Every month, NRBs wire lakhs of taka home for land purchases. Too many discover later that the deed was forged, the seller had no title, or a sibling quietly sold the same plot to someone else. A fraud ring recently exposed in Dhaka used 6 fake NIDs and 50 bank accounts, with insiders at the registry office facilitating over Tk 50 crore in fraudulent transactions. The 2023 Land Crime Prevention Act now carries up to 7 years imprisonment for fraudulent sellers, but prevention beats prosecution.

This guide walks you through the complete **land registration process Bangladesh step by step**, written specifically for Non-Resident Bangladeshis. Steps 1 through 3 can be completed entirely from abroad using government portals like eporcha.gov.bd and dolil.gov.bd. Steps 4 through 7 require either your physical presence or a registered Special Power of Attorney. NRBs can purchase residential and commercial land freely. Agricultural land is restricted unless inherited. Follow these seven steps, and you will have a registered deed, a mutated khatian, and peace of mind.

## Step 1: Verify the Land Title Online Using eporcha.gov.bd

Before you spend a single taka or engage a lawyer, run a free title check from your living room. The government portal eporcha.gov.bd provides 24/7 access to khatian records (records of rights) across Bangladesh. No registration or login is required for basic searches.

### How to Search

Go to eporcha.gov.bd and select the following fields in order: Division, District, Upazila, and Mouza. A mouza is the smallest revenue unit of land in Bangladesh, roughly equivalent to a village or neighborhood. Enter either the khatian number (the record of rights number assigned to the owner) or the dag number (the plot number on the survey map). Searching by dag number is more reliable if the seller has provided plot details.

### What to Look For

Confirm that the seller's name appears in the current khatian. Under Section 53C of the Transfer of Property Act, any sale by a person whose name does not appear in the current khatian is legally void. If the khatian shows multiple owners, you are dealing with a joint khatian, which signals inheritance complications. Every co-owner must consent to the sale, or the transaction is invalid.

### Limitations

The portal displays RS, SA, and BS khatians but may not reflect very recent mutations. Treat eporcha as a first filter, not a substitute for a full title search by a qualified lawyer.

### NRB Tip

Screenshot and save every record you pull. If the seller claims the khatian is "being updated," treat that as a red flag. This is a common stalling tactic designed to buy time while the seller arranges a fake or outdated document.

**Milestone:** You now have a preliminary title verification and screenshots of the khatian records confirming the seller's name.

## Step 2: Hire a Local Lawyer and Complete Due Diligence

Non-negotiable: do not rely on relatives or brokers (dalals) for due diligence. NRBs on social media regularly describe being defrauded by trusted family members. Inheritance fraud, where siblings forge heirship certificates omitting the NRB heir, is a documented and recurring pattern. A lawyer with no personal stake in the transaction is your only reliable safeguard.

### What the Lawyer Should Verify

A proper title search covers the full chain of ownership for a minimum of 25 years. Never accept a seller-provided draft or khosra khatian as proof. The lawyer should also conduct a pending litigation search at the relevant court, verify mutation records at the AC Land office, obtain an encumbrance certificate, check Duplicate Carbon Receipts (DCR) for prior transactions, and physically inspect the land.

### Documents to Demand from the Seller

Request the original deed (dolil), khatian, National ID, TIN certificate (mandatory for any transaction exceeding BDT 100,000), mutation certificate, rent receipts (khajna), and heirship certificate if the seller inherited the property.

### Remote Coordination

Your lawyer can share documents via WhatsApp or email for your review. Video calls for property walkthroughs work well. The critical point is that you are receiving verified originals or certified copies, not scans of unverified documents.

### Red Flags

The seller refuses to provide original documents. The seller pressures you with artificial urgency. The price is far below market value. Any of these should halt the process immediately.

**Milestone:** You have a lawyer's written opinion confirming clean title, a complete document set from the seller, and no pending litigation.

## Step 3: Open an NRTA Account and Transfer Funds

If you transfer purchase money through a regular savings account or through hundi (informal remittance), you permanently lose the legal right to repatriate sale proceeds if you ever sell the property. Bangladesh Bank rules are strict on this point, and retroactive verification is impossible.

### NRTA Account Explained

A Non-Resident Taka Account (NRTA) must be opened at an authorized dealer bank in Bangladesh. This is not the same as a NITA (Non-Resident Investor's Taka Account, which is for securities). The NRB can open an NRTA remotely through the bank's foreign branch or correspondent bank.

### SWIFT Message Requirement

The remittance SWIFT message must explicitly state: "Investment for purchase of land at [full address including mouza and district]." Generic descriptions like "family support" or "gift" will disqualify the transaction from future repatriation eligibility. Get this right the first time. There is no retroactive correction.

### Encashment Certificate

Upon receipt of funds, the bank issues an Encashment Certificate. Keep the original in a secure location. This is your sole proof that foreign currency was converted through proper banking channels. It will be required if you ever sell the property and want to repatriate the proceeds.

### NRB Remittance Incentive

Bangladesh Bank currently offers a 2.5% cash incentive on inward remittances channeled through officially licensed money transfer operators and banking channels. This applies to NRBs remitting capital for property purchases. Use official channels, not hundi, and you recover part of the transfer cost immediately.

### How Much to Send

Transfer enough for the full purchase price plus all fees. Stamp duty, registration fees, local government tax, source tax, and mutation fees collectively run 7 to 10 percent of the deed value. A buffer of 10 to 15 percent above the agreed price is prudent.

**Milestone:** You have a funded NRTA account and an Encashment Certificate proving legitimate remittance.

## Step 4: Execute a Special Power of Attorney from Abroad

If you cannot be physically present at the Sub-Registrar office for deed execution and biometric verification, you need a Special Power of Attorney. Not a General PoA. A Special PoA must specifically authorize the attorney to execute the sale deed, pay all fees, and complete registration for the identified property.

### Step-by-Step PoA Process

1. **Draft the PoA with your Bangladesh lawyer.** Include specific property details: dag number, khatian number, mouza, and every authorized action.
2. **Visit the Bangladesh Embassy or Consulate in person.** This is mandatory. There is no postal or online option. Bring your passport, NID (if available), and the drafted PoA. The embassy attests it. Fees vary by country, typically $20 to $50 USD.
3. **Send the attested PoA to Bangladesh.** The attorney-in-fact must obtain MoFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) counter-attestation within 2 months of the embassy attestation date. Miss this deadline and the attestation expires. Start over.
4. **District Commissioner attestation.** After MoFA, the PoA goes to the DC office for another round of verification.
5. **Register the PoA at the local Sub-Registrar office.** This takes 7 to 10 days.

### Timeline

The total chain from embassy to registered PoA runs 3 to 6 weeks if expedited. Plan accordingly.

### Critical Warning

An unregistered PoA has no legal standing for land registration. The Sub-Registrar office will reject it outright during biometric verification. Do not let anyone tell you otherwise.

If you plan to visit Bangladesh for the signing, you can skip this step entirely and appear in person at the Sub-Registrar office.

**Milestone:** You have a fully registered Special PoA authorizing your attorney to act on your behalf at the Sub-Registrar office.

## Step 5: Sign a Registered Baina Deed to Lock In the Property

An unregistered baina deed has no legal force in court. If you pay an advance based on a handshake or an unregistered agreement and the seller sells to someone else, you have no legal recourse. This happens frequently to NRBs who are thousands of miles away and cannot respond quickly.

### What a Baina Deed Is

A baina deed is a preliminary agreement that locks in the sale price, records the advance payment amount, and commits both parties to completing the final sale within a specified period. Think of it as a binding commitment with penalty clauses.

### Why Registration Matters

Only a registered baina deed is enforceable. It must be executed on non-judicial stamp paper and registered at the Sub-Registrar office. Include the full property description (dag, khatian, mouza), agreed sale price, advance amount, completion deadline, and penalty clauses for breach by either party.

Your attorney, via the registered PoA from Step 4, can execute and register the baina deed on your behalf while you remain abroad. This is especially useful when there is a gap of weeks or months before the final sale deed.

**Milestone:** You have a registered baina deed protecting your advance payment and locking the seller into the agreed terms.

## Step 6: Calculate Fees, Execute the Sale Deed, and Register at the Sub-Registrar Office

Ownership transfers at the Sub-Registrar office through biometric verification and fee payment totaling 7 to 10 percent of the deed value.

### Fee Breakdown (Current Rates)

| Fee | Rate |
|---|---|
| Stamp duty | 1.5% of deed value |
| Registration fee | 1% of deed value |
| Local government tax | 2 to 3% (varies by municipality/union) |
| Source tax | 2 to 5% (varies by mouza) |
| Fixed fees (e-fee, petition, notice, cartridge paper) | Approximately Tk 420-600 |
| **Total** | **Roughly 7 to 10% of deed value** |

Check dolil.gov.bd to find the minimum government-assessed value (mouza rate) for the specific plot. The deed value cannot be declared below this rate. The source tax rate also varies significantly by mouza, so verify the exact percentage on the portal before calculating.

### Documents Required at the Sub-Registrar Office

Both parties (or their attorneys) must present: the sale deed drafted by the lawyer and printed on stamp paper, NIDs, TIN certificates, passport-size photographs, khatian copy, mutation certificate, rent receipts, PoA if applicable, and the Encashment Certificate for the NRB buyer.

### What Happens at the Office

The deed is presented to the Sub-Registrar. Fees are paid via bank challan. Biometric fingerprint scans are taken of both the buyer (or attorney) and the seller. The Sub-Registrar reviews the documents, signs the deed, enters it in the registry book, and issues a receipt. Same-day registration is common for straightforward cases. Complex matters may take 2 to 3 days.

The biometric requirement is mandatory. This is precisely why an unregistered PoA gets rejected at this stage. The system requires the PoA holder's biometrics to be on file.

### E-Registration (Coming 2026)

The Registration (Amendment) Ordinance 2026 introduces digital e-registration, allowing deeds to be presented, verified, and registered electronically. Electronic biometric verification and automated workflows will replace paper-based systems. As of late 2026, the rollout is underway across Sub-Registrar offices but adoption is uneven. Confirm with your lawyer whether the relevant office has implemented e-registration before your appointment, as the procedure may vary.

**Milestone:** You hold a registered sale deed. Legal ownership has transferred to your name.

## Step 7: Complete Mutation (Namjari) and Update the Land Records

Registration transfers legal ownership, but without mutation the government land records still show the previous owner. This creates real problems when you try to sell, obtain a loan, or even pay land tax. Mutation is not optional.

### What Mutation Is

Namjari is the process of updating the khatian at the AC Land office to reflect the new owner's name. It is a separate administrative step from registration, handled by a different office.

### How to Apply

Submit an application to the Assistant Commissioner (Land) office with the registered deed, khatian copy, DCR, and NID. The fee is Tk 1,170. The statutory deadline for processing is 45 days, or 60 days in metropolitan areas. In practice, delays are common.

### If Mutation Is Rejected

The appeal chain proceeds as follows:

1. Appeal to the Additional Deputy Commissioner (ADC)
2. Then to the Additional Divisional Commissioner (ADivC)
3. Then to the Land Appeal Board
4. Finally, the High Court

Each level has its own timeline and fees. Your lawyer can handle the entire appeal process remotely on your behalf.

### Post-Mutation

Pay land tax (khajna) promptly under your name. Unpaid tax can trigger adverse possession claims over time. Your PoA holder can complete the mutation process on your behalf. Insist on receiving a certified copy of the updated khatian as proof of completed mutation.

**Milestone:** The government land records now reflect your name as the owner. You have a certified copy of the updated khatian and are current on land tax.

## Can Foreign Nationals (Non-Bangladeshis) Buy Land in Bangladesh?

The rules for foreign nationals differ significantly from those for NRBs. The short answer: direct land ownership by foreign individuals is prohibited under the Land Holding Limitation Order 1972 and the Foreign Private Investment (Promotion and Protection) Act 1980. However, Bangladesh offers structured legal pathways for foreign investment in real estate and industry.

### What Foreign Individuals Can and Cannot Buy

| Property Type | Foreign Individual | Foreign-Owned Company |
|---|---|---|
| Residential land | Not permitted | Permitted (with BIDA approval) |
| Commercial land | Not permitted | Permitted (with BIDA approval) |
| Agricultural land | Not permitted | Not permitted |
| Apartments / flats | Permitted | Permitted |
| Commercial spaces | Permitted | Permitted |
| Government (khas) land | Not permitted | Not permitted |

Foreign individuals have been permitted to purchase apartments and commercial spaces directly since 1996. Payment must be made in foreign currency. All purchases require a Bangladesh TIN, BIDA approval, and notification of the local police station.

### How Foreign Nationals Buy Land for Factories or Investment

Foreign nationals who wish to own land for a factory, warehouse, or manufacturing plant cannot do so in their personal name. The legal route is to establish a Bangladesh-registered company.

**Corporate route for land ownership:**

1. Register a private limited company with the Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms (RJSC). Bangladesh permits 100% foreign ownership, so no local partner is required.
2. Obtain BIDA (Bangladesh Investment Development Authority) project registration with land-use authorization for the intended purpose.
3. The company, once incorporated as a Bangladesh legal entity, can purchase or hold land in its own name with the same rights as a domestic company.
4. Additional approvals required: Bangladesh Bank (for foreign currency investment), National Board of Revenue (TIN for the company), and Ministry of Land (for agricultural or government land, which is generally unavailable to private investors).

**Important RAJUK caveat:** RAJUK (Rajdhani Unnayan Kartripakkha), which controls land development in greater Dhaka, does not sell plots directly to foreign nationals. This applies even when purchasing through a company in some circumstances. Engage a Bangladeshi lawyer before assuming the corporate route removes all restrictions.

### BEPZA and Special Economic Zones

Foreign investors establishing factories or industrial units do not need to buy land at all to operate lawfully. Two more direct routes exist:

**BEPZA (Bangladesh Export Processing Zones Authority):** BEPZA operates Export Processing Zones across Bangladesh. Foreign investors can lease fully serviced industrial plots on a 30-year lease basis, with renewal options. Standard Factory Buildings (SFBs) are also available on 2-year renewable leases. BEPZA allows Type A (100% foreign ownership) enterprises. Registration fee is USD 500 with one year's rent as a security deposit. No separate BIDA approval is needed for BEPZA-licensed entities. Recent examples: DJ Copper Co Ltd and GRX Technology (BD) Co Ltd signed BEPZA lease agreements in November 2025 for a combined investment of USD 70.66 million.

**SEZs (Special Economic Zones):** Bangladesh Economic Zones Authority (BEZA) operates special economic zones with similar land-lease arrangements for manufacturing and industrial investment. Terms and incentives vary by zone.

For most foreign investors building a factory, the BEPZA or SEZ lease route is faster, legally simpler, and comes with pre-built infrastructure compared to navigating private land purchase through a local company.

### Required Approvals for Foreign Company Land Purchase

If the corporate route (private land purchase) is chosen over a BEPZA/SEZ lease, the approvals required are:

- BIDA project registration
- Bangladesh Bank approval (for foreign currency investment)
- TIN from the National Board of Revenue
- Ministry of Land approval (for certain land categories)
- Directorate of Registration (Sub-Registrar office)
- Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, and Board Resolution from RJSC

The process is significantly more complex and time-consuming than the NRB route. Specialized legal counsel with investment law experience is essential.

## Frequently Asked Questions About NRB Land Registration in Bangladesh

### Can I buy agricultural land in Bangladesh as an NRB?

Only if inherited. NRBs cannot directly purchase agricultural land. Residential and commercial land purchases are permitted without restriction.

### What are the most common fraud schemes targeting NRBs?

Three patterns dominate: inheritance fraud using forged heirship certificates that omit the NRB heir, double sales using fake NIDs to sell the same plot to multiple buyers, and fake khas (government) land sales where the seller claims state-owned land as private property.

### Do I need a TIN to buy land in Bangladesh?

Yes. Both buyer and seller must have a TIN for any transaction exceeding BDT 100,000. Obtain yours before initiating the purchase.

### Can I complete the entire registration process without visiting Bangladesh?

Yes, provided you execute a registered Special PoA as described in Step 4. The only in-person requirement is visiting a Bangladesh Embassy or Consulate abroad for PoA attestation.

### What is the 2023 Land Crime Prevention Act?

This law introduced penalties of up to 7 years imprisonment for fraudulent land sellers and stronger legal recourse for victims, including NRBs.

### How much does it cost for a foreigner to buy property in Bangladesh?

For a resale apartment, budget approximately 7–8% of the purchase price in taxes and fees (stamp duty 4% in Dhaka, registration fee 1%, local government tax 2%, plus fixed charges). A new apartment from a developer adds 4% VAT, pushing the total to 11–12%. For land purchased through a Bangladesh-registered company, the deed fees run 7–10% of the land value, plus one-time corporate setup costs of roughly Tk 1–3 lakh. See the full breakdown in the Cost Breakdown section above.

### Can a foreign national (non-Bangladeshi) buy land in Bangladesh?

Not directly. Foreign individuals are prohibited from registering land in their personal name under the Land Holding Limitation Order 1972. They may purchase apartments and commercial spaces. To own land for a factory or business, they must register a Bangladesh company (100% foreign ownership is permitted), obtain BIDA project registration, and purchase through the company. For industrial use, leasing land through BEPZA or a Special Economic Zone is often faster and legally simpler.

### Is e-registration available in Bangladesh now?

The Registration (Amendment) Ordinance 2026 introduced a legal framework for digital e-registration, electronic biometric verification, and automated deed processing. Rollout is ongoing across Sub-Registrar offices. Confirm with your lawyer whether the specific office handling your deed has implemented e-registration, as many offices are still transitioning from the paper-based process.
